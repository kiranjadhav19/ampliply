In one sentence. Ampliply runs no servers, so our apps send nothing to us: your settings stay on your phone, and the only third party involved is Google, which serves the ads that keep the apps free and which we ask your consent for where the law requires it.

1. The short version

QuestionAnswer
Do you have an account system?No. There is nothing to sign up for in any Ampliply app.
Do you collect my personal data?No. We operate no backend, so there is nowhere for it to go.
Do you have analytics or crash reporting of your own?No.

How do I delete everything?Uninstall the app. Android removes everything it saved.

2. Who we are

Ampliply is an independent developer of Android apps, publishing on Google Play. In this policy “we”, “us” and “Ampliply” mean the developer of the apps listed on this site. For data protection purposes we are the controller of the limited processing described below.

Write to us at support@ampliply.com about anything in this policy, including a request about your rights.

3. What this policy covers

This policy applies to the website at ampliply.com and to every app we publish. Because our apps differ in what they do on your device, each has a supplement that spells out its own storage and permissions:

  • TV Remote for Samsung Smart TV — com.kiranjadhav.tvremote
  • Unplug: Auto Offline — app.unplug.autooffline

Where a supplement and this master policy differ, the supplement wins for that app. Everything not addressed in a supplement is governed by this page.

4. What we do not do

It is easier to be specific about the absences, so:

  • We do not ask you to create an account, sign in or give us an email address to use an app.
  • We do not run servers that receive data from our apps. There is no Ampliply backend.
  • We do not embed analytics, attribution, A/B‑testing, heatmap, session‑replay or crash‑reporting SDKs.
  • We do not read your contacts, photos, files, messages, call logs, calendar, microphone, camera or precise location, and our apps do not request those permissions.
  • We do not sell, rent or trade personal information, and we do not share it with data brokers.
  • We do not build profiles about you or combine data across our apps.
  • We do not send marketing email. There is no mailing list.

5. Data our apps keep on your device

Our apps save the few things they need in order to be useful the next time you open them. This data is held in the app’s private storage on your phone, under Android’s own sandbox. We cannot see it, we never receive a copy of it, and Android deletes it when you uninstall the app.

Typically this means your settings and preferences, the devices or items you have deliberately saved inside the app, and simple local counters such as a daily total. The exact list for each app is in its supplement.

Because this data never reaches us, we are not able to access, export or delete it on your behalf — but you can, at any time, from inside the app or by uninstalling it.

6. Advertising

Our apps are free and are funded by advertising. We use Google AdMob and the Google Mobile Ads SDK is embedded in the apps. This is the one circumstance in which data leaves your device.

To select, deliver, measure and protect those ads from fraud, Google may process:

DataPurpose
Advertising ID (a resettable device identifier)Ad selection, frequency capping, measurement, fraud prevention
Device and app information (model, OS version, app version, language, screen size)Rendering ads correctly, measurement
IP address, from which an approximate location is derivedServing locally relevant ads, legal compliance, fraud prevention
Ad interactions (impressions, clicks, time to dismiss)Measurement and billing
Diagnostics about the ad SDK (errors, performance)Keeping ads working

Google acts as an independent controller for much of this processing. How it uses the data is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and in the Google Privacy Policy.

Your consent, and how to change it

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app shows Google’s consent form (the User Messaging Platform) before any personalised advertising happens, and records your answer. You can change that answer at any time: open the app’s menu or settings and choose Ad privacy options (or Privacy options). If you decline personalised ads, the app keeps working and shows non‑personalised ads instead.

Everywhere, you can also limit ad tracking on the device itself: Android Settings → Google → All services → Ads, where you can delete or reset your advertising ID or turn off ad personalisation entirely. Google’s own controls are at My Ad Center.

Advertising and “selling” or “sharing” under US state law

We do not sell personal information for money. Showing personalised ads through AdMob may, however, count as “sharing” for cross‑context behavioural advertising under California’s CPRA and similar laws in other states. Using Ad privacy options in the app, or turning off ad personalisation in your Android settings, is how you opt out. You may also email us and we will confirm what applies to your device.

7. This website

ampliply.com is a set of static pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, carries no advertising, has no comment system and has no login. Nothing you do here is tracked or profiled.

Two things are worth knowing. First, the site loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, which means your browser requests those font files from Google and your IP address is visible to Google in the process; no cookie is set by that request. Second, like any website, the host that serves these pages processes the technical request data needed to deliver them — IP address, user agent, the page requested and the time — in short server logs used for security and reliability. We do not combine that with anything else or use it to identify you.

If you email us, we hold that email in our mailbox for as long as we need it to answer you and to keep a record of the conversation, and no longer.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access the personal data held about you, to have it corrected or erased, to object to or restrict processing, to data portability, to withdraw consent at any time, and not to be discriminated against for exercising any of this.

In practice, for Ampliply apps:

  • Erasure of app data: uninstall the app, or use the app’s own clear or forget control. This is immediate and complete, and does not need us.
  • Advertising data: we do not hold it — Google does. Use Ad privacy options in the app, your Android ad settings, or Google’s own tools to reset or delete it.
  • Everything else: email support@ampliply.com. We answer rights requests within 30 days, and we will tell you honestly when the answer is that we hold nothing about you — which, for app usage, it almost always is.

If you are in the EEA or UK and are unhappy with how we have handled a request, you can complain to your national data protection authority. We would rather you told us first.

11. Children

Our apps are not directed at children. They are rated for a general audience but, because they show personalised advertising and are not built to the Google Play Families policy, they are intended for users aged 13 and over (16 in some countries, or the equivalent minimum age where you live). We do not knowingly collect anything from a child. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, email us and we will delete it.

12. International transfers

We hold no database, so we transfer nothing ourselves. Google operates globally and may process advertising data outside your country, including in the United States, relying on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses and other approved safeguards. Details are in Google’s own privacy documentation.

14. Security

Not holding data is the strongest security measure available to us, and it is the one we rely on most. Beyond that: traffic between an app and Google’s ad services is encrypted in transit; app data on your device is protected by Android’s per‑app sandbox and your device encryption; and where an app talks to hardware on your own network, it does so locally without any cloud relay. No system is perfect, and we will tell you plainly and promptly if something ever goes wrong that affects you.

15. Changes to this policy

If we add a feature or an SDK that changes what is described here, we will update this page and change the “last updated” date before the change reaches you in a release. Material changes will also be noted in the app’s release notes on Google Play. Continuing to use an app after an update means you accept the revised policy; if you would rather not, uninstalling removes everything.
