If you read one section, read the next one. Unplug uses Android’s VPN feature, and you are right to be careful about any app that does. It is a local VPN with no server behind it, and it exists only to stop traffic leaving your phone — not to carry it somewhere.

The VPN, in plain words

Android does not allow an app to switch Wi‑Fi or mobile data off. The only way to pause a phone’s internet from inside an app is to become the device’s VPN and then not forward anything. That is exactly what Unplug does, and nothing more.

  • The VPN has no remote server and no endpoint. There is nowhere for traffic to go.
  • Packets that reach it are discarded on your device. They are not read, inspected, parsed, logged, stored, modified, forwarded, proxied or shared with anyone, including us.
  • We never see your browsing, your DNS lookups, the apps you use or the sites you visit. Not in aggregate, not anonymised, not at all — we have no mechanism that could receive them.
  • When you are offline, the only exception is any app you have explicitly added to Always allowed apps, which Android then routes outside the VPN as normal.
  • Android shows a key icon in your status bar whenever any VPN is active. You will see it while Unplug is holding your phone offline. That is Android telling you the truth, and we would rather you noticed it.

Android permits only one active VPN at a time. If you start another VPN app, Android switches Unplug’s offline mode off, and Unplug tells you that is what happened.

What is stored on your phone

All of it is in the app’s private storage, inside Android’s per‑app sandbox, and none of it is transmitted anywhere.

The installed apps list

When you open Always allowed apps, the app asks Android for the apps that appear in your launcher so it can show you a list to choose from. This matters, so to be exact:

  • The list is read only while that screen is open, and only to draw it.
  • It is not stored and not sent anywhere.
  • The only thing saved is the package names you deliberately tick, and they stay on your device.

Advertising in this app

The free app shows a Google AdMob banner. The data Google processes for advertising is listed in the master policy, and in the EEA, UK and Switzerland the consent form appears before any personalised advertising.

To change your answer later, open the app’s menu and choose Ad privacy options.

One practical note: while your phone is offline, ads cannot load either. That is expected — the app works the same way with or without them.

Deleting your data

  • Stop the VPN: tap Go online, or switch Auto‑Offline off. You can also revoke VPN permission entirely in Android Settings → Network & internet → VPN.
  • Everything the app saved: uninstall it, or use Android Settings → Apps → Unplug → Storage → Clear data.
  • Advertising data held by Google: reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings → Google → All services → Ads.
