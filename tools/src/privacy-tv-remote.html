The point of this app is that it does not need the internet. It talks to your television across your own home network. Your key presses go from your phone to your TV and no further — there is no cloud relay, no account and no server of ours in the path.

Summary

QuestionAnswer
Does it know what I watch?No. It sends key presses; it does not read the channel, the programme or the app on screen.
Does my TV’s address leave my phone?No. Saved TVs stay in the app’s private storage.
Is there an account?No, and there is no sign‑in to skip.
Does anything leave the device at all?Only advertising requests to Google, described below.

What is stored on your phone

All of it lives in the app’s private storage, inside Android’s per‑app sandbox. None of it is transmitted to us or to anyone else.

WhatWhy
The TVs you have connected to — the name the TV reports, its local IP address and MAC addressSo the app can reconnect without you searching again, and can send a Wake‑on‑LAN packet to power the TV on
The pairing token your TV issues the first time you allow the appSo your TV does not ask you to allow the app again on every connection
Which saved TV was used lastSo the app opens straight onto the right remote
Your preferences — haptics on or off, volume‑button behaviour, and similarSo the app behaves the way you set it

A local IP address, a MAC address and a pairing token are device identifiers for equipment inside your home. Because they are never transmitted off your phone, they are not “collected” under Google Play’s definition — but we list them here so you know exactly what the app holds.

What travels on your network

Two kinds of traffic, and they are easy to separate:

  • To your TV, on your local network. Discovery messages used to find Samsung televisions on the network, a WebSocket connection to the TV for key presses, and a Wake‑on‑LAN packet when you power the TV on. All of this stays inside your network. The TV’s connection is TLS‑protected and the app pins the certificate it expects.
  • To Google, over the internet. Advertising requests, and the consent form where it applies. Nothing else.

There is no third destination. The app has no telemetry endpoint, no update check of its own and no “phone home” on launch.

That is the complete list. There are no runtime permission prompts, because the app asks for nothing sensitive: no location, no contacts, no storage, no camera, no microphone, no phone state.

On location. The app never requests location access. It reads your Wi‑Fi subnet to scan for televisions, which is not your location and is not shared. Separately, Google may derive an approximate location from your IP address when serving ads — that is Google’s processing, described in the master policy.

Advertising in this app

The app is free and shows Google AdMob ads: an anchored banner, an occasional full‑screen interstitial and an app‑open ad when you return after being away. The data Google processes for this is listed in the master policy, and in the EEA, UK and Switzerland the consent form appears before any personalised advertising.

To change your answer later, open Settings → Ad privacy choices in the app.

We also pace the ads deliberately, which is a product decision rather than a privacy one but is worth stating: no full‑screen ad while you are pressing buttons, never over pairing, dialogs, settings, help or the TV list, and never two full‑screen ads within ninety seconds of each other.

Deleting your data

  • One TV: open the TV list and tap Forget. Its address and pairing token are removed immediately.
  • Everything: uninstall the app, or use Android Settings → Apps → TV Remote → Storage → Clear data. Both remove all of the above.
  • Advertising data held by Google: reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings → Google → All services → Ads.

Your television may also hold a record that it granted a device access. Clear it from the TV’s own settings — on most Samsung sets, General → External Device Manager → Device Connect Manager → Device List.
